# Growth Playbooks

Reference frameworks for `campaign-planner` and anyone planning a launch or a community push.
Distinct from `context/campaign-planning.md`, which is sequencing rules for the day-by-day content
calendar — this file is strategic frameworks to draw on when a plan needs one. Extracted and
tightened from a general-purpose marketing skill pack; kept only the parts genuinely relevant to a
small team's social/launch/community work, not the parts assuming a marketing org with a CRO, ads,
and email team.

## The ORB framework — for a launch or major campaign

Every launch's channels split into three types. The goal is always the same regardless of channel:
convert borrowed and rented attention into an owned relationship.

- **Owned** (email list, blog, this Notion pipeline, a branded Slack/Discord): you control it fully,
  no algorithm risk, compounds over time. Weakest at first, strongest eventually.
- **Rented** (LinkedIn, X, Reddit, YouTube): real reach, but the platform owns the algorithm. Use it
  to drive people toward an owned channel, not as the whole strategy.
- **Borrowed** (a guest post, a podcast interview, an influencer who covers you unprompted): instant
  credibility, shortcuts the "getting noticed" problem, but only pays off if it converts into an
  owned relationship afterward.

A launch plan that's 100% rented-channel posts with no path to an owned channel (a waitlist, a
follow, a signup) is leaving the compounding value on the table.

## Five-phase launch shape

Not every ship needs all five phases — this is the shape for a genuinely major launch (a new
product tier, a headline feature), not a routine ship.

1. **Internal** — friendly users test for free, validate core functionality.
2. **Alpha** — landing page + early access signup, invite people individually.
3. **Beta** — broader early access, first public teasers about the problem being solved (not the
   product yet).
4. **Early access** — screenshots, demos, GIFs go public; gather real usage data.
5. **Full launch** — self-serve open, announcement across every channel at once (customer emails,
   in-app, blog, social, Show HN / Product Hunt if relevant).

## Launch-day checklist (the day itself)

- [ ] Announcement live on every owned channel simultaneously
- [ ] Social posts scheduled/posted across LinkedIn, X (Reddit only if a genuine fit — see
      `context/reddit-style.md`)
- [ ] Team ready to respond to every comment/question in real time, especially the first hour —
      early velocity is what gets a post pushed to non-followers, on every platform that matters
      here
- [ ] Traffic has somewhere to land that converts (a page, a signup, not just "look at this")

## Content repurposing — turning one thing into many

The fastest way to fill a content calendar without inventing five separate ideas: extract "atoms"
from one longer piece (a demo, a workshop, a founder call, a benchmark writeup) rather than
starting from a blank page for every post.

| Atom type | What it looks like | Best platform |
|---|---|---|
| Quotable moment | A bold claim or memorable line, 15-60s of source material | X, LinkedIn |
| Story arc | A complete mini-story: setup, conflict, resolution | Video (`/video`), LinkedIn |
| Tactical tip | One specific how-to explained clearly | LinkedIn, a short video |
| Controversial take | A contrarian opinion that invites real disagreement | X, LinkedIn |
| Data/stat callout | A surprising confirmed number (never an unconfirmed one — see hard-rules.md) | LinkedIn |

Workflow: pick the 5-10 best moments from the source material, write each as a **standalone**
caption (don't assume the reader saw the rest), adapt format per platform, spread across 1-2 weeks
rather than dumping them all the same day.

## Community flywheel — if a Ratel community push comes up

Communities compound when the loop closes: members join → get real value → engage → create content
or help others → new members discover it from that content. Every community decision should ask
whether it accelerates or slows that loop.

- **Build around identity, not the product.** People join because of what Ratel does, they stay
  because of who else is there and what being a member says about them.
- **The first 20-50 members are recruited manually**, not through a public opening. Don't launch a
  community publicly until there's baseline activity to walk into.
- **1% of members generate most of the value.** Find them, give them real recognition (not just
  "early access"), and the rest of the community follows their lead.

Reddit specifically: `context/reddit-style.md` already covers subreddit selection and voice for
individual posts. This flywheel model is for a genuine owned-community push (a Ratel Discord/Slack),
which is a bigger commitment than posting to existing subreddits — don't conflate the two.
